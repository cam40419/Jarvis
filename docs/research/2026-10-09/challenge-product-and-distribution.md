# Product, buyer and distribution challenge

Second iteration, **October 9, 2026**. This challenges the [opportunity catalog](opportunity-map.md), [workforce research](workforce-platforms.md) and [enterprise comparison](enterprise-and-infrastructure.md). Strategies, customer problems, commercial thresholds and channel behavior below are hypotheses. No customer interview, paid pilot, vendor contact or comparative production trial occurred. No product code or authoritative roadmap changed.

## What changed after the first pass

The first pass favored accepted project delivery, consequence-aware operations and a brand/agency workspace. This pass narrows the purchase to **one repeated job and one person whose budget or capacity improves**. Technical confidence matters only if that person prefers the result to their current assistant, shared folder, board and human operator.

Specialist proofing weakens the proposed acceptance distinction further. Ziflow documents decisions and reviewer progress per proof version, unique version IDs, a brief, staged decision rights and locks. Filestage documents version approval, text/version comparison, timestamped exportable decisions and reviewer-group isolation. Neither inspected source establishes Simon's entire proposed current-source/authority/repair/cost contract; both establish substantial acceptance functionality. The defensible question is reduced supervision and rework across a changing production loop. [Ziflow proof details](https://help.ziflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/30721821727124-About-Proof-Details), [workflow rules](https://help.ziflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/37922335934996-Add-reviewers-and-configure-workflows-for-a-proof), [Filestage decisions](https://help.filestage.io/en/articles/2562896-submit-your-review-decision), [comparison and records](https://help.filestage.io/en/articles/9113215-how-to-verify-that-everyone-s-feedback-has-been-met).

Guest review is also established: Filestage supports account-free feedback, limited reviewer-group visibility and optional free reviewer dashboards. A review link can be a useful adoption channel, but that channel alone is easy to match. [Reviewer access](https://help.filestage.io/en/articles/9112521-how-to-share-content-with-reviewers-and-control-their-permissions).

Simon currently has bounded artifact/review and native execution contracts, rather than the proposed complete production business. Automatic review is bounded UTF-8, generic files have a 1 MiB limit, and accepted business adapters, rich visual/Office validation and a complete hosted release remain gaps. The clothing-brand pilot is an available learning setting, not verified market demand. [Artifact runbook](../../runbooks/native-artifacts.md), [execution runbook](../../runbooks/native-execution.md), [roadmap](../../next-phases.md).

## Four credible entry strategies

**1. Human-assisted campaign delivery for a small agency.** Buyer: agency owner or delivery lead. First job: a source-backed messaging/product-copy packet, approved corrections and handoff across a few related assets. Begin with supported text; richer assets require accepted adapters and validators. The offer is reduced producer/reviewer time and consistent updates, not autonomous art direction. A practitioner-led concierge package can test demand before self-service. Compare against that agency's producer, strong assistant and proofing stack. Kill this entry if three paid repeated jobs cannot show capacity benefit after including expert review and support, or buyers value only bespoke human consulting. This is the leading near-term learning route, subject to capability gates.

**2. Acceptance and repair beside existing agents.** Buyer: AI-enabled agency/product operations lead already paying for agents and a board. Simon imports a named output, checks supported requirements/evidence, routes a correction and exports the accepted packet. Begin with one import and one handoff path; leave task and credential authority explicit. Distribution: practitioner implementation partners and a before/after demonstration on their real repeated job. Charge for managed active workflows or project capacity, with transparent usage. Kill if incumbent configuration matches the result with less administration, or synchronizing two systems causes more confusion than the reviewer removes. This is the strongest product-shaped alternative to replacing the board.

**3. Maintained research-to-decision dossier.** Buyer: a small product/operations team making a recurring supplier or market decision. Offer bounded evidence updates, contradiction handling, changed recommendations and a human decision record. Distribution: specialists who already prepare that report, beginning with a paid maintenance experiment rather than a broad research subscription. Compare against their researcher using a strong research assistant and document. Kill if source-change impact is unreliable, decisions rarely repeat, or buyers pay for expert interpretation while upkeep has negligible value. Avoid claiming domain expertise or suitability for high-stakes decisions without separate validation.

**4. Private workstation companion for a supported production job.** Buyer: an owner with an explicit data/application constraint, not merely a preference for “local.” Offer a narrow customer-controlled file workflow with verified data destinations, editable outputs and honest setup/support requirements. Distribution: a specialist who can support that environment. A paid deployment/support offer is more plausible initially than a cheap mass-market subscription. Kill if the selected model/tool boundary cannot be verified, quality trails the buyer's permitted alternatives, or support/hardware burden destroys value. Local inference and remote runners must be assessed separately; current Simon settings are not a demonstrated end-to-end private deployment. [Model controls](../../runbooks/project-models.md).

These are competing entry hypotheses. Choose one buyer/job before building their union. Consequential operations becomes an expansion option only after one accepted adapter and real recovery evidence; enterprise governance, regulated review and manufacturing guarantees are separate businesses.

## Three bold ideas for bounded prototypes

1. **A scope-change negotiator.** On one synthetic campaign with ten requirements and three deliverables, change two facts and one owner preference. Produce a minimal repair plan and proposed budget/deadline/approval changes. Compare with a human producer; stop if material impact is missed or scope negotiation becomes slower. No automatic contractual or financial action.
2. **A cross-provider handoff that stays useful.** Move one text project from an outside maker to Simon review, a human receiver and a second maker. Export exact files, historical decisions and unresolved work; re-establish permissions at each boundary. Seed revoked access and altered bytes. Stop if recipients must reconstruct the record or the packet implies transferable authority.
3. **A fixed-scope completion offer with a repair reserve.** Before selling anything, replay held-out jobs and price an included correction allowance against p90 execution/review/support cost. Then test a small human-assisted paid offer with explicit exclusions and capped scope, if separately authorized. Stop if acceptable margin requires hiding failure costs or quietly weakening acceptance. This tests a commercial promise, rather than inventing another credit unit.

## Pricing, willingness to pay and switching tests

No willingness-to-pay evidence exists here. A proposed price floor includes model/tool/runtime charges, unsuccessful attempts, p90 support, domain review and onboarding amortization. The plausible ceiling is the avoidable cost of the buyer's next-best option. Saved minutes create economic value only if they reduce expense or enable valuable additional work. Recording approved outcomes does not justify a premium without that link.

Avoid unlimited work and guarantees whose correctness or third-party consequences Simon cannot control. Outcome-only charging already has competitors, including Asana's completed-request packaging; it is a tariff hypothesis rather than a distinction. [Asana pricing](https://asana.com/pricing). Separate the platform/service fee, included allowance, third-party bills, overruns, correction limits, external reviewers and export rights. Do not convert competing credit units into fictitious task costs.

For the chosen entry, propose six interviews based on two recent completed jobs each, then three paid repeat pilots. These are analyst-selected experiment sizes, not statistically validated market thresholds. Record payer, current purchase, avoidable defects, active review minutes, rework, collaborator friction, repeat use and total supported cost. A possible continuation rule is at least 30% less active supervision with no worse blind defect result, no required control failures, and positive contribution margin. Pre-register the exact task and thresholds; change them transparently if evidence shows the wrong metric.

Reduce switching by shipping an export into the existing workflow before requiring project migration. Import only necessary records; make data permissions and object ownership explicit. Test the client/reviewer experience as seriously as the maker experience. Interviews and pilots are proposed next evidence, not actions performed or authorization to contact anyone.

## Pruning and remaining uncertainty

Discard as headline claims: more agents, persistent memory, second-agent review, model choice, BYOK, generic approvals, versions, signatures, dashboards and local hosting. They may be useful components; substantial comparators already exist. Defer universal company operation, an agent marketplace, proprietary integration breadth, mass enterprise control planes and regulated assurances until a specific buyer funds their obligations.

The substantive unresolved questions are which repeated job hurts enough to buy, whether source/requirement impact can be accurate and quiet, whether improved review survives stronger competitor configuration, whether human partners can distribute it profitably, and whether supported outcomes repeat with manageable onboarding. The next pass should acquire that evidence, not enlarge the feature catalog indefinitely. Keep an avenue only when new evidence could change the buyer, build/integrate choice, pricing or proof obligation.
